How the reference photo steers the image

The 🖼️ Vision Encode node shows the reference photo to Qwen3.5-9B, the same model that reads your text. Its vision tower turns the photo into image tokens placed right before your words, so Kromoss gets one conditioning that holds both. No pixels from the photo are reused: Kromoss draws a brand-new photo from noise.

Getting the most out of it

  • It's strong. Outfit, hair, jewelry and even background props carry over and beat text that disagrees. Write only what should change.
  • Crop the reference tight on the person if you want her look but not the room.
  • image_weight is a weak dial. It fades the photo toward gray before the encoder, and at 0.25 the outfit still came through. Use the 🖼️ REFERENCE switch to turn it off.
  • Keep the KSampler's negative wired to Vision Encode and its negative_prompt empty. That negative carries the photo too; in testing, a plain empty negative made CFG 3 copy the reference even harder.
  • Use the W4A8 text encoder from the downloads: it includes the vision tower. FP8 text encoders can't run the vision path.

Pipeline A in action

👁️ Spot a bad run early and cancel it

Every pipeline has a LIVE PREVIEW group with KJNodes' Model Preview Override. It shows the image while it's being generated, not only at the end.

Why it matters

  • Don't wait for a render you'll throw away. At step 5 the picture is still a blur, but the pose, the framing, the cap and the setting already read.
  • Cancel the moment something's off: a wrong pose, a bad crop, a prop leaking in from the reference. Press ✕ next to Run, fix the prompt or seed, and go again.
  • It adds up. Here cancelling would save the ~96 s the node estimates are left. Over a session of tries, that's a lot of waiting saved.
  • By about 30% of the steps the face is readable too.

The strip under the preview shows the step, the seconds per step and the time left, plus the noise curve and the time each step took.

Anatomy of the prompt

The LoRA was trained on one single caption. Keep it word for word and only edit the two sentences after it.

head_swap: start with <image1> as the base image, keeping its lighting, environment, and background, her outfit, the camo cap on her head and the ramen bowl on the table. remove the head from <image1> completely and replace it with the head from <image2>, strictly preserving the hair, eye color, nose structure from <image2>. change the facial expression: she is laughing hard in a genuine burst of laughter, eyes squeezed almost shut into happy crescents, cheeks pushed up, eyebrows lifted, no pursed lips. change the pose: the hand raising the middle finger moves to her face and covers her mouth and chin as she laughs, palm toward her face, fingers relaxed and slightly spread, her eyes, nose and cheeks stay visible above the hand; her head tips slightly forward and to one side, face still turned toward the camera; the other hand keeps holding the chopsticks with noodles above the bowl. the face stays the same size and position in the frame, skin texture and lighting on the face and hand matching the scene, natural hand with five fingers, high quality, sharp details, 4k

The prompt used for this result

The LoRA's training caption, word for word, with three things written for this shot: what to keep from Image 1, a body trait to take from Image 2, and which expression to keep.

head_swap: start with <image1> as the base image, keeping its lighting, environment, and background, her outfit, the camo cap on her head and the ramen bowl on the table. remove the head from <image1> completely and replace it with the head from <image2>, strictly preserving the hair, eye color, nose structure from <image2>. Also give <image1> the large round breasts from <image2> keep the duck face facial expression, the face stays the same size and position in the frame, skin texture and lighting on the face and hand matching the scene, natural hand with five fingers, high quality, sharp details, 4k
training caption + quality tail: keepwritten for this shot

Why each part is there

  • head_swap: opens the one caption the LoRA was trained on. Keeping its wording is what the LoRA recognizes.
  • "keeping its lighting… and background, her outfit, the camo cap…" lists everything in Image 1 that must survive. Name the items that touch the head (hats, glasses, earrings) or they may be swapped away.
  • "remove the head from <image1>… replace it with the head from <image2>" sets the direction: Image 1 is the body, Image 2 is the face. Swap the images and you swap who's who.
  • "Also give <image1> the large round breasts from <image2>" reaches past the head. BFS only swaps heads, so this part is Qwen 2.1's own editing following the instruction. It can carry a body trait over from Image 2, but it's less locked-down than the head swap: name the exact trait you want moved.
  • "keep the duck face facial expression" takes the place of the caption's copy the direction of the eye, head rotation, micro expressions from <image1>. Either works for keeping the pose; to change the expression instead, use the change the facial expression: sentence from the section above.
  • The quality tail keeps the head at the same size and spot, and matches skin and light to the scene so the face doesn't look pasted on.

Pipeline B in action: a head swap on the canvas

Do it yourself

  1. Toggles: A off, B on.
  2. Image 1: the shot you want: body, outfit, pose, place.
  3. Image 2: the face you want. Large, sharp, roughly front-facing, evenly lit, nothing over the eyes or nose. Its hair comes along, so pick a hairstyle you're happy with.
  4. Prompt: keep the caption; in the first sentence, list what to keep from Image 1 (especially hats or glasses); then either keep the expression or change it.
  5. Negative: remove anything you're asking to keep.
  6. Run and check the hairline, the neck seam and leftover hair. If it's off, try another seed or a closer head angle between the two images.

Only swap faces you have the rights to: your own AI characters, or people who gave consent. Never public figures.

Recipe: a carousel from one shot

  1. A  Generate the hero shot (A on, B off). That's slide 1.
  2. B  Load it as Image 1 and Image 2, turn A off and B on, and write a new expression and pose. Each prompt is another slide with the same girl in the same place.
